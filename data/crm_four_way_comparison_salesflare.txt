We compared Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho CRM, Pipedrive and Salesflare hands-on, then rechecked their pricing, AI capabilities and current user ratings so you can see the practical differences without opening five trials at once.

The short answer: choose Salesforce if you need deep enterprise customization and can support a heavier implementation; HubSpot if you want sales, marketing and service in one ecosystem; Zoho CRM if you want a very broad feature set at a lower price; Pipedrive if you mainly want straightforward visual pipeline management; and Salesflare if you run a B2B sales team and want the CRM to keep itself up to date with as little manual admin as possible.

Still building your shortlist beyond these five? See our best sales CRM comparison for a wider ranking focused on pipelines, lead follow-up and sales automation.

I co-founded Salesflare, so I am not a neutral observer, but I am a real expert. To make the comparison maximally useful, I use the same criteria for every CRM, link to current pricing sources, include current G2 user ratings, and preserve the findings of an independent sales professional who tested four of these CRMs side-by-side.

Salesforce logo

Salesforce
Best for: large or complex organizations that need deep customization, governance and enterprise reporting.
Avoid if: you want a lightweight CRM that a small sales team can administer itself.
Practical price in this comparison: Core at $195/user/month billed annually, before many optional AI and service add-ons.

Background & positioning
Salesforce was founded in 1999 and helped popularize the idea of running CRM software in the cloud. Early on it served many smaller companies, but over time its center of gravity moved toward larger organizations with more complex requirements.

That history explains the product you see today. Salesforce is less a narrowly defined sales tool and more a highly configurable platform. Its biggest strength is that you can model unusually complex processes, data structures, permissions and workflows. The flip side is that larger implementations often involve admins, consultants and ongoing governance.

Salesforce now also offers Starter and Pro entry points, but Core is where more of the advanced flexibility, API access and AI options become relevant.

Pricing & total cost
Salesforce lists Free Suite at $0, Starter Suite at $25, Pro Suite at $100, Core at $195, Advanced at $395 and Max at $550 per user per month. Starter offers monthly or annual billing; the other listed rates are billed annually.

For a serious comparison with the other CRMs here, Core is the more realistic reference point if you need advanced pipeline management, API access and broader automation. The higher editions and sales engagement add-ons bundle different AI capabilities. Implementation and ongoing administration can add materially to the software price.

Ease of setup & use
Salesforce’s interface has become much more modern over the years, but the fundamental design trade-off remains: it prioritizes flexibility and configuration over simplicity. That is ideal when a CRM must adapt to a complex organization, but it creates more screens, settings and decisions than a typical small sales team needs.

Salesforce account interface

Sales features & pipeline
Salesforce is the deepest product in this comparison for complex sales operations. It supports lead, account, contact and opportunity management, advanced forecasting, pipeline inspection, quotes, products, approval processes, territories and extensive role/permission models.

Email & integrations
Salesforce integrates with Gmail and Outlook and has by far the largest ecosystem in this comparison through AppExchange. Automatic email, event and contact sync is included in Starter Suite. Advanced sales engagement and API access depend on the edition and add-ons.

Automation & AI
Salesforce offers the broadest enterprise AI platform in this group, but it is also the most packaging-dependent. Depending on edition and add-ons, its sales AI can include lead and opportunity scoring, call summaries, sales signals, conversation intelligence, predictive forecasting, Agentforce actions and more.

In our hands-on AI CRM testing, the most visible use cases included data enrichment, call summaries and insights, drafting emails, lead scoring and relationship intelligence. The strategic upside is enormous if your Salesforce data is complete and your organization has the resources to configure the platform well. The practical downside is that cost, data quality and implementation complexity matter a lot.

Mobile
Salesforce provides a full mobile app, and its current Sales Cloud comparison lists offline mobile functionality across editions. It is a capable mobile CRM, although the underlying Salesforce process complexity does not disappear simply because the screen is smaller.

Customization & reporting
Winner of this comparison. If your priority is building a CRM around a complex organization rather than adapting your organization to the CRM, Salesforce has the deepest toolkit here.

Support
Standard self-service resources are included. Premier Success costs 30% of net license fees when purchased separately and is included in Core, Advanced and Max. It adds expert guidance and 24/7 support for business-stopping issues. Bigger Salesforce deployments commonly rely on internal admins and implementation partners as well.

Pros and cons
Pros: exceptional customization, very broad sales functionality, deep reporting, huge ecosystem, enterprise governance.
Cons: expensive, complex, heavier implementation and administration, many advanced capabilities are separately packaged.
Verdict
Choose Salesforce when complexity is a requirement, not an accident. If you have unusual processes, multiple business units, strict governance needs and the budget to implement it properly, it is difficult to beat. If your main goal is getting a small sales team productive quickly, it is usually more CRM than you need.

HubSpot logo

HubSpot
Best for: companies that want sales, marketing, service and content tools in one connected ecosystem.
Avoid if: you only need a focused sales CRM and want predictable low pricing as your requirements grow.
Practical price in this comparison: Sales Hub Professional at $90/seat/month billed annually plus required onboarding.

Background & positioning
HubSpot started as a marketing automation company before launching its CRM and sales platform in 2014. It has since expanded into an all-in-one customer platform spanning marketing, sales, service, content, data and commerce.

That history explains both the strength and the trade-off of HubSpot. It is attractive when several go-to-market teams want one connected platform, but a sales team buying HubSpot is also buying into a much broader product architecture than a dedicated sales CRM.

Pricing & total cost
HubSpot lists Sales Hub Professional at $90/seat/month billed annually and Enterprise at $150. Professional has a required $1,500 onboarding fee; Enterprise requires $3,500, as specified in HubSpot’s product and services catalog. Some AI functionality consumes HubSpot credits, and additional credit packages can add to the monthly bill.

Starter can be appealing for basic use, but Professional is the more realistic comparison point if you want custom workflows, stronger reporting and the broader automation that makes Sales Hub competitive with the other full-featured products here.

Ease of setup & use
HubSpot is generally easier to start with than Salesforce and less feature-dense than Zoho CRM. The interface is polished, but the navigation has grown as HubSpot has added more hubs and more functionality.

HubSpot sales dashboard

Sales features & pipeline
Sales Hub covers deals, tasks, sequences, forecasting, playbooks, reporting and conversation intelligence. It is especially attractive when sales activity needs to connect closely with HubSpot Marketing Hub or Service Hub.

Email & integrations
HubSpot’s Gmail and Outlook integrations are strong and its marketplace now spans thousands of apps. Email tracking, templates, sequences and scheduling are well integrated into the broader sales workflow, although access to advanced features depends on plan.

Automation & AI
HubSpot’s AI offering is centered around Breeze and AI features across its customer platform. In our hands-on testing, useful sales features included CRM timeline summaries, a chat-style interface and email drafting. HubSpot has also been adding prospecting and data tools that use HubSpot credits.

The main advantage is breadth: AI can sit across marketing, sales and service data in the HubSpot ecosystem. The trade-off is that AI usage, plan requirements and credits make the total package more complicated than the headline CRM price suggests.

Mobile
HubSpot’s mobile app handles the core CRM workflow well, including contacts, companies, deals and tasks. As with most broad suites, the deepest configuration and reporting work is more comfortable on desktop.

Customization & reporting
HubSpot offers a good level of customization and reporting for SMB and mid-market teams, especially on Professional and Enterprise. It is less open-ended than Salesforce but easier to configure.

Support
Free users mainly rely on the community and knowledge base. Starter adds chat and email support; Professional and Enterprise add phone support. Direct support requires an eligible paid seat. Professional onboarding is charged separately and does not replace implementation work.

Pros and cons
Pros: excellent all-in-one ecosystem, strong marketing-sales connection, polished interface, large integration marketplace, broad AI investment.
Cons: advanced sales functionality gets expensive, required onboarding on Professional, broader suite can feel heavy for a sales-only team.
Verdict
Choose HubSpot when the CRM is part of a broader decision to bring sales, marketing and service together. If sales is the only problem you are solving, compare the full Professional-level cost with a focused sales CRM before deciding.

Zoho CRM logo

Zoho CRM
Best for: teams that want a lot of functionality and customization for relatively little money.
Avoid if: simplicity and a clean day-to-day sales experience are your top priorities.
Practical price in this comparison: Enterprise at $40/user/month billed annually if you want the broader Zia AI feature set.

Background & positioning
Zoho Corporation has been building business software since the 1990s and launched Zoho CRM in 2005. Unlike vendors focused on one category, Zoho builds an enormous range of apps across sales, marketing, finance, HR, support, productivity and IT.

In CRM, Zoho has historically positioned itself as a lower-cost alternative to Salesforce. That DNA is still visible: the product tries to cover a very wide range of requirements, while the surrounding Zoho ecosystem gives you many adjacent applications without enterprise-level Salesforce pricing.

Pricing & total cost
Zoho CRM currently lists Standard at $14/user/month billed annually, Professional at $23, Enterprise at $40 and Ultimate at $52. Monthly billing is $20, $35, $50 and $65 respectively.

Professional is a strong value point for traditional CRM functionality. For this comparison I use Enterprise because it includes the broader set of Zia AI capabilities. Zoho Premium Support adds 20% to the subscription if you want that service level.

Zoho’s feature list includes 2 customer-facing data backups per month on Standard, Professional and Enterprise, and 4 on Ultimate. Additional backups are charged per request. This is separate from Zoho’s own disaster-recovery arrangements.

Ease of setup & use
Zoho CRM’s breadth is also its biggest usability challenge. It exposes a lot of navigation, fields, modules, options and configuration. If you like being able to tweak almost everything, that can be a strength; if you want a CRM that feels obvious from day one, it can be overwhelming.

Zoho CRM dashboard

Sales features & pipeline
Zoho CRM covers leads, accounts, contacts, deals, forecasting, scoring, assignment rules, products, quotes, inventory-related workflows and many adjacent functions. It can support surprisingly complex processes for the price.

Email & integrations
Zoho CRM integrates with Google Workspace and Microsoft email environments and benefits from the large Zoho application suite. Its edition comparison includes email integration from Standard and email intelligence from Professional. Buying Zoho CRM does not include every Zoho app.

Automation & AI
Zoho’s Zia assistant has been around for years. On Enterprise, Zoho currently advertises AI capabilities including anomaly detection, churn prediction, best-time-to-contact, record-owner suggestions, workflow suggestions, prompt building, Zia Agents and Zia Agent Studio.

In our hands-on AI test, we also found useful data enrichment, email-thread summarization, call transcription and predictive/autofill functionality. The recurring trade-off was that several useful operations still required more clicks and manual configuration than we would ideally want from an intelligent CRM.

Mobile
Zoho offers mobile CRM apps that cover the core records and activities. Its strength remains breadth; the experience is more feature-heavy than the simpler sales CRMs in this comparison.

Customization & reporting
Zoho is second only to Salesforce here. It offers extensive modules, fields, views, automation, reporting and adjacent apps at a much lower list price.

Support
Paid editions include Classic support for 8 hours a day, 5 days a week. Zoho Premium Support adds 20% to the subscription for 24/5 availability and a 3-hour initial response. Enterprise Support costs 25% of annual subscription fees and requires at least 50 user licenses. A faster initial response is not a guaranteed resolution time.

Pros and cons
Pros: huge feature set, strong value, deep customization, broad business-app ecosystem, mature Zia AI.
Cons: busy interface, more setup and navigation, some intelligent workflows still require manual steps.
Verdict
Choose Zoho when feature breadth per dollar matters more than elegance. It is particularly compelling for teams willing to invest some time configuring their CRM and potentially adopt other Zoho apps as well.

Pipedrive logo

Pipedrive
Best for: sales teams that want an intuitive visual pipeline and a focused sales CRM.
Avoid if: your main goal is to minimize manual CRM upkeep through automatic relationship data capture.
Practical price in this comparison: Growth at $39/seat/month billed annually; Premium at $59 if you want deeper AI, enrichment and full-cycle sales features.

Background & positioning
Pipedrive was founded by salespeople who were frustrated with enterprise CRMs built more for management than for the reps using them. Its defining idea was a simple visual pipeline that made deal management obvious.

Over time Pipedrive has added email sync, automation, sequences, forecasting, enrichment, AI and more advanced plans, but the visual pipeline remains the center of the product. That makes it a very different proposition from Salesforce, HubSpot or Zoho.

Pricing & total cost
Pipedrive currently lists Lite at $14/seat/month billed annually, Growth at $39, Premium at $59 and Ultimate at $79. Growth is where full email sync, tracking, automations and nurturing sequences become part of the package. Premium adds features such as custom scoring, enrichment and more AI-powered sales tools.

Ease of setup & use
Pipedrive is one of the easiest CRMs in this comparison to understand. The deal pipeline is visual, lists are straightforward and the product stays focused on sales rather than trying to become an all-department suite.

Pipedrive deals pipeline

Sales features & pipeline
This is Pipedrive’s core strength. Leads, deals, activities, products, forecasts and sales automations are built around progressing opportunities through a pipeline. For teams migrating from spreadsheets or a heavier CRM, the mental model is easy to grasp.

Email & integrations
Growth adds full Gmail, Outlook or company-email sync, open/link tracking, bulk emails and nurturing sequences. The integration is much stronger than Pipedrive’s earlier generations, although the product still expects users to actively maintain more CRM data than Salesflare does. Open and click tracking requires sending from Pipedrive; synchronizing an inbox does not add tracking to messages sent from Gmail or Outlook.

Pipedrive’s automation limits are 50 active automations on Growth, 150 on Premium and 250 on Ultimate, per company. Automated email actions share a 40-per-minute company limit, in addition to the email provider’s limits.

Automation & AI
Pipedrive now includes AI across its plans in different forms. Lite includes AI-assisted report creation; higher plans add more sales-assistant and email capabilities. In our hands-on AI testing, we explored next-best-action suggestions, email drafting, email-thread summaries and AI report creation.

These are useful additions to a traditional sales CRM. The main distinction versus Salesflare is architectural: Pipedrive largely adds intelligence on top of a CRM that users maintain, while Salesflare’s core proposition starts with capturing much of the relationship data automatically.

Mobile
Pipedrive has mature iOS and Android apps for managing leads, contacts, deals and activities on the go. As with most CRMs, desktop remains the more natural environment for deeper administration and reporting.

Customization & reporting
Pipedrive offers enough fields, pipelines, automations and reporting for many SMB sales teams, especially on Premium and Ultimate. It is deliberately less open-ended than Salesforce or Zoho.

Support
Pipedrive provides email and 24/7 live chat support. Phone support is available on Premium and Ultimate for users with account-settings access. English phone support runs Monday to Friday, 7 am to 11 pm CET/CEST.

Pros and cons
Pros: excellent visual pipeline, easy to learn, sales-focused, strong email/automation improvements, sensible mid-market feature set.
Cons: more active data upkeep than an automation-first CRM, deeper features move you into higher plans, less suitable for complex enterprise-wide processes.
Verdict
Choose Pipedrive if the thing you most want from a CRM is a clear, usable sales pipeline. It remains one of the safest choices for a team that wants traditional CRM discipline without Salesforce-level complexity.

Salesflare logo

Salesflare
Best for: small and medium-sized B2B sales teams that want less CRM admin through automatic customer data and activity capture.
Avoid if: you want one suite for marketing, service, ERP-like workflows and highly customized enterprise processes.
Practical price in this comparison: Growth at $29/user/month billed annually, including all current intelligent features.

Background & positioning
Salesflare was founded in 2014 after my co-founder and I became frustrated with the amount of data entry required by the CRMs we tried. We realized most of the information a sales team manually enters already exists in email, calendars, email signatures, meetings, web tracking and public data.

That became the product’s core position: a B2B sales CRM that automatically collects and organizes relationship data, then uses that data to help the team follow up and sell. In other words, the starting point is not “how do we make data entry easier?” but “how much data entry can we remove?”

Pricing & total cost
Salesflare Growth costs $29/user/month billed annually or $39 monthly. Pro is $49 annually or $64 monthly and adds advanced sequences, permissions and custom dashboards. Enterprise is $99 annually or $124 monthly with a five-user minimum and adds setup, migration, training and a dedicated account manager.

All of the intelligent features discussed below are available on Growth, so there is no separate AI plan or AI add-on required for them.

Ease of setup & use
The key usability difference is that Salesflare starts populating itself as soon as you connect your email and calendar. It suggests accounts and contacts, imports relationship history, builds timelines and gives you a working sales pipeline without requiring a large data-entry project first.

Salesflare opportunities pipeline

Sales features & pipeline
Salesflare is built specifically around B2B account-based sales. It combines accounts, contacts, opportunities, tasks, sequences, team relationships, dashboards and activity timelines, while automatically logging the customer interactions that keep those records useful.

Email & integrations
Salesflare works directly inside Gmail and Outlook through its email sidebars, while also synchronizing emails, meetings and contacts in the background. Email opens, link clicks and website visits can be tracked regardless of whether you work from the inbox, desktop app or mobile app. It also connects with Zapier, Make and other integration platforms.

Automation & AI
Salesflare’s intelligence is embedded into the core CRM rather than presented as one separate assistant. Current capabilities include:

Suggested accounts and contacts based on who your team is actually communicating with.
Automatic data enrichment from email signatures, meetings and public data.
Timeline summaries, next-step suggestions and Q&A using the full relationship history.
Follow-up, email reply and meeting-note reminders when important actions are being forgotten.
Relationship intelligence that shows who on your team knows a company or contact best.
Lead engagement scoring based on real activity such as emails, clicks and website visits.
Contextual email writing that can use your history with the contact, company/account context, opportunity data and your personal writing style.
The underlying bet is simple: useful CRM AI needs good relationship data. Salesflare has spent most of its history automating that data foundation first, then building AI on top of it.

Mobile
Salesflare is designed to keep the full core CRM experience available across desktop, mobile and the Gmail/Outlook sidebars. Its current pricing page explicitly positions the mobile app and email sidebars as ways to use Salesflare without losing desktop features.

Customization & reporting
Salesflare supports custom fields, pipelines, workflows, filters, dashboards and permissions, with more advanced controls on Pro. It intentionally stops short of the open-ended platform customization of Salesforce or Zoho.

Support
Human chat and email support is included, with team calls available when a problem is better solved live. Enterprise adds dedicated onboarding, migration and account management.

Pros and cons
Pros: minimal manual data entry, deep Gmail/Outlook workflow, strong B2B relationship context, useful embedded AI, simple pricing, easy setup.
Cons: narrower than an all-in-one suite, less suitable for highly customized enterprise processes, not intended to replace marketing/service/ERP platforms.
Verdict
If you run an active B2B sales team and the reason your CRM fails is that people do not keep it updated, Salesflare is the strongest fit in this comparison. If you need a broad cross-department suite or highly bespoke enterprise workflows, HubSpot or Salesforce will make more sense.

The questions buyers ask when comparing these CRMs
Which CRM has the best AI?
It depends on what you mean by “best AI.” Salesforce offers the broadest enterprise AI platform and the most room to build sophisticated AI-assisted processes, but it can get expensive and packaging-dependent. HubSpot is compelling if you want AI across a connected marketing, sales and service platform. Zoho has a mature Zia feature set at a comparatively low price. Pipedrive adds useful AI to a simple sales workflow.

For a B2B sales team that mainly wants AI to understand actual relationship history and reduce CRM administration, Salesflare is the one I would shortlist first: its AI sits on top of automatically captured emails, meetings, contacts, account data and opportunity context. For more detail, see our full hands-on AI CRM comparison.

Which CRM is easiest to implement?
Salesflare and Pipedrive are the easiest to get going with. Pipedrive wins on the simplicity of a traditional visual sales pipeline. Salesflare goes one step further on setup by automatically deriving accounts, contacts and activity history from connected email/calendar data. HubSpot is relatively approachable but gets more complex as you adopt more hubs. Zoho needs more configuration because of its breadth. Salesforce is the heaviest implementation in this group.

Which CRM has the best email integration?
For teams that want the CRM to live naturally inside Gmail or Outlook, Salesflare is the strongest fit in our testing because its inbox sidebars, automatic activity capture and tracking are part of the core product architecture. HubSpot also has a strong email workflow. Pipedrive’s full email sync and tracking start on Growth. Salesforce and Zoho both integrate with Gmail and Outlook, but their broader product experience is less centered around working from the inbox.

Which CRM is best for a small B2B sales team?
Salesflare is the most purpose-built option here for a small B2B sales team that wants low CRM administration. Pipedrive is a strong alternative if your priority is simple pipeline management. HubSpot makes more sense if sales needs to share one platform with marketing and service. Zoho is attractive when budget and feature breadth matter most. Salesforce is usually difficult to justify for a small team unless its process requirements are unusually complex.

Which CRM is most customizable?
Salesforce wins clearly, followed by Zoho CRM. HubSpot provides substantial customization without demanding the same implementation overhead. Pipedrive and Salesflare deliberately trade some platform-level flexibility for a more focused sales experience.

Bottom line: HubSpot is the broader suite; Pipedrive is the more focused sales tool.

HubSpot vs Salesflare: which is better?
Choose HubSpot if you want one vendor for sales, marketing, service and content.
Choose Salesflare if you want a focused B2B sales CRM that automatically captures relationship data and works deeply from Gmail or Outlook.
Bottom line: HubSpot wins on suite breadth; Salesflare wins when reducing B2B sales CRM admin is the main goal.

Pipedrive vs Salesflare: which is better?
Choose Pipedrive if your top priority is a straightforward, visual pipeline with a traditional CRM workflow.
Choose Salesflare if you want the same sales focus but more automatic contact/account creation, relationship history, reminders and inbox-native workflow.
Bottom line: both are sales-focused and easy to use; the key difference is how much of the CRM you want software to maintain for you.

Also considered: Microsoft Dynamics 365 and Freshsales
Some CRM comparison searches also surface Microsoft Dynamics 365 Sales and Freshsales, so they are worth mentioning even though this article focuses on the five products above.

Conclusion: which CRM wins?
There is no useful single winner without knowing what you are trying to optimize.

Best for enterprise customization: Salesforce
Best all-in-one customer platform: HubSpot
Best feature breadth for the price: Zoho CRM
Best straightforward visual sales pipeline: Pipedrive
Best for minimizing B2B sales CRM admin: Salesflare
For a small or midsize B2B sales team, I would start with Salesflare when the priority is keeping customer data complete with minimal manual work. Pipedrive is the closest alternative for a simpler visual pipeline; HubSpot, Zoho and Salesforce earn their place when your wider ecosystem or process requirements need them.
